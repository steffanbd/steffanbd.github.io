Special Projects

Problems worth building a product around

Two concepts sketched out at business-plan depth — each one a real operational problem I've lived, worked through as if I were going to build it.

These are theoretical concepts — a place to think in public about product and business-model design. They are not active ventures, and no company exists behind either one yet.
01 Facilities & Space Data

Concord

One source of truth for space data — so a mismatch surfaces on a Tuesday, not during an audit.

The problem

Facilities teams run space and asset data in Archibus (or a similar IWMS) while HR and finance run Workday, and service requests flow through ServiceNow. The three don't talk to each other natively. Reconciling them is a recurring, manual chore — and a mis-coded department or a stale cost center usually surfaces only when leadership asks a question the data can't cleanly answer.

The idea

A lightweight reconciliation and visibility layer that sits alongside existing systems rather than replacing them: pulls space, org, and ticket data on a schedule, flags mismatches automatically, and gives the space-planning team one dashboard for utilization, budget, and data-quality health.

1
Connect

Read-only connectors to Archibus, Workday, and ServiceNow; nightly sync into a staging database; a diff engine flags department, cost-center, and location mismatches.

2
Reconcile

A review queue where an analyst confirms or corrects each flag, with a full audit trail and an exportable data dictionary — the same kind of governance package built at AU.

3
Visualize & alert

Utilization and budget dashboards, scheduled reports to leadership, and proactive alerts — so the mismatch surfaces before an audit or capital cycle finds it first.

Who it's for

Mid-size university facilities/space-planning offices and multi-site enterprise real-estate teams — big enough to run an IWMS plus an HRIS, too small for a dedicated data-integration team.

Business model

Annual subscription priced per building or per seat, sized for a facilities-office budget rather than an enterprise IT budget, plus a one-time connector implementation fee.

Why this, why me

Built and maintained exactly this reconciliation by hand for 3+ years — knows which mismatches actually matter and what a real audit asks for.

Open question: API access varies a lot by an institution's Archibus/Workday licensing tier — this likely needs an implementation-services layer at first, until enough customers let the connectors standardize.
02 People & Compensation Analytics

Compa

Pay-band and pay-equity analysis for HR teams too small for a dedicated comp function.

The problem

Most organizations under ~500 employees don't have a dedicated compensation analyst. Pay decisions get made ad hoc in spreadsheets, with no consistent way to check an offer against a pay band, spot compression, or answer a pay-equity question before it becomes a legal one.

The idea

A tool that ingests an HRIS export — pay, role, level, location, tenure — and produces the pay-band, compa-ratio, and equity views a comp analyst would build by hand, without needing an enterprise HRIS's advanced-compensation module.

1
Ingest & structure

Guided CSV upload and column mapping; automatic pay-band suggestions by role, level, and geography using percentile bands.

2
Analyze

Compa-ratio pivot views, compression flags (a senior underpaid relative to a newer junior hire), and a pay-equity summary by role and level.

3
Recommend & track

Suggested adjustment ranges within a budget the HR lead sets, plus year-over-year tracking so the same analysis takes minutes at the next cycle.

Who it's for

HR generalists and HR Business Partners without a comp specialist — the person doing this in a spreadsheet, under deadline, once a year at review time.

Business model

Per-seat subscription priced for an HR admin, not per employee, to stay affordable at this org size — with a free tier capped at one analysis a year.

Why this, why me

Built exactly this compensation-analytics tool by hand at AMI Expeditionary Healthcare, and it's the tool HR Business Partners actually adopted and used.

Open question: handling pay-equity/demographic data responsibly is a real compliance surface, not just a UX problem — this needs legal and compliance input before touching protected-class analysis, not just an engineering decision.
